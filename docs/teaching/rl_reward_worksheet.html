In this repo, the robot's total reward each timestep is a sum of small terms, each with a weight that says how much it matters. Below are the terms that are actually turned on in walk.gin. A positive weight rewards a behavior; a term whose name starts with penalty_ (or is naturally negative) discourages a behavior.

Part 1 — Read the reward function

For each term, fill in the two blank columns using the plain-English description as a clue.

Reward term Weight What it measures (given) What behavior does it encourage? What might break if you set its weight to 0?
lin_vel_xy2.0How well the robot's actual forward/sideways speed matches the commanded walking speed.  
ang_vel_z1.5How well the robot's turning rate matches the commanded turn.  
torso_quat2.5How close the torso's orientation is to upright.  
feet_phase7.5Whether each foot is at the right height at the right moment in the step cycle (lift, then plant).  
alive1.0A small bonus given every timestep the robot has not fallen.  
penalty_action_rate2.0How much the motor commands jump around from one step to the next.  
penalty_ang_vel_xy1.0How much the torso is rolling/pitching (wobbling side-to-side or front-to-back).  
penalty_pose0.5How far the leg joints drift from their natural default resting angles.  
penalty_close_feet_xy10.0Whether the two feet cross over or get dangerously close together.  
penalty_feet_ori5.0Whether the feet are tilted (on tip-toe) instead of flat on the ground.  
Instructor answer key (click to reveal)
  • lin_vel_xy — walk at the speed it's told; without it the robot has no reason to move forward at all (may just stand).
  • ang_vel_z — turn when told; without it, turn commands are ignored.
  • torso_quat — stay upright; without it, it may lean or topple as long as other terms are met.
  • feet_phase — pick up its feet and take real steps; without it, it tends to shuffle/drag feet or slide.
  • alive — survive rather than fall early; without it, less pressure to avoid ending the episode.
  • penalty_action_rate — smooth motions; without it, jittery/twitchy commands (bad for real motors).
  • penalty_ang_vel_xy — stop wobbling; without it, rocking/unstable gait.
  • penalty_pose — keep a natural posture; without it, weird contorted joint angles that still "work" in sim.
  • penalty_close_feet_xy — don't trip over its own feet; without it, feet cross and it stumbles.
  • penalty_feet_ori — keep feet flat; without it, tip-toe walking (fragile, unrealistic).

Key idea to draw out: agents optimize exactly what you reward, not what you meant. Remove a term and the policy will happily exploit the gap ("reward hacking").

Part 3 — Run it

Edit the config, then launch a short classroom training run (layers the scaled-down budget on top of walk.gin):

python toddlerbot/locomotion/train_mjx.py --env walk --gin-file "walk walk_class"

To change a reward weight without editing files, you can override it on the command line, e.g. turn off the upright reward:

python toddlerbot/locomotion/train_mjx.py --env walk --gin-file "walk walk_class" \
  --gin-config "RewardScales.torso_quat = 0.0"
This is a scaled-down run (~10–15 min) — it will improve but not fully converge. Compare its reward curve and video against the baseline run and the pre-trained policy.
